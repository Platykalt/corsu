# Why Corsu exists

[Français](../fr/pourquoi-corsu.md)

## Learning a language, speaking it, using it

Learning a language, being able to speak it and having occasions to use it are three different things. A language
is passed on mostly when people meet it in ordinary situations, where it feels normal and does not have to be
chosen over another one.

Many Corsicans, especially on the coast and in towns, have nowhere to practise Corsican outside school. They are
also the people who spend the most time online. That does not take away their wish to learn the language or their
attachment to it. And many speakers rarely find an occasion to use Corsican, because it feels out of place in the
situations they are in.

## The example of Irish

Irish shows the gap between knowing a language and using it. In the 2022 census of the Republic of Ireland,
1,873,997 people said they could speak Irish, 6% more than in 2016. Yet only 71,968 people said they spoke it every
day outside the education system, a number down by about 2% over the same period
([Central Statistics Office, Census of Population 2022, Profile 8](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp8/censusofpopulation2022profile8-theirishlanguageandeducation/irishlanguageandthegaeltacht/)).

These figures do not mean that Irish is only spoken at school, nor that the gap has a single cause. Each person's
level, the chances to hold a conversation and social habits all play a part. They only show that a language many
people have learned can still be little used in daily life.

## Corsu's two goals

1. **Make Corsican familiar every day.** The menus, buttons and settings people look at daily become a way to meet
   Corsican words and phrasing in context. This passive learning adds to lessons and conversation; it does not
   replace them.
2. **Give Corsican more places where it feels natural.** Computers and phones are where modern life happens most.
   If Corsican has its place there, speakers find their language in what they do every day, and learners come across
   it without opening a textbook.

The idea is simple: passing on a language works better when there are real occasions to use it. Corsu tries to
create some by giving Corsican an ordinary place in digital life.

A translated interface is not a conversation. Corsu can help people get used to the language, learn it and feel
entitled to use it. It does not replace exchanges between speakers, and it cannot revive a language on its own.

## Practising with someone

If you have nobody to speak Corsican with, these tools let you read, write and hold a conversation in Corsican:

- [Sapienzia](https://www.sapienzia.io): French-Corsican translation, a shared dictionary, conjugation and a
  conversational assistant.
- [Astutu](https://astutu.corsica): a conversational assistant that answers in Corsican.
- [LIV](https://liv.corsica): an artificial intelligence that speaks Corsican.
